Profit curve — GP2 and GP3 against cost

Each dot is one simulated target ROAS. GP3 peaks where the GP2 curve's slope falls to 1.0 — past that point Google can still buy more GP2, but each extra krona of spend buys less than a krona of it, so GP3 falls. When an incrementality factor below 1.0 applies, the incremental GP3 line is the one the recommendation is read off; the observed GP3 line stays visible next to it.

Marginal ROAS by target

ΔGP2 ÷ ΔCost between adjacent simulation points, plotted at each segment's midpoint target. Where the line crosses 1.0 is the profit-maximising target; the crossing is found by linear interpolation between the two bracketing segments. With an incrementality factor below 1.0 the solid line is the incremental marginal (ΔiGP2 ÷ ΔCost) — the one that decides — and the faint dashed line is the observed marginal it was cut down from.

Equal-marginal-return allocation

Spend the budget one krona at a time, always on whichever strategy returns the most incremental GP2 for it. At the stopping point every strategy earns the same marginal incremental ROAS — the condition for a profit-optimal portfolio. Below that, targets are read back off each strategy's curve. Because brand and private-label value is discounted first, this is what pulls budget out of them and into generic; the note below sizes that shift against the unadjusted allocation.
